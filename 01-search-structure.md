# 01 · Search campaign structure

## Structure by intent

```
Account
├── Brand            people searching your name
├── High intent      "[service] agency", "hire [service]", "[service] cost"
├── Problem          "how to [solve problem]", "[problem] fix"
├── Competitor       (optional) competitor names, separate budget
└── Generic / test   broader category terms, tight budget
```

Each campaign gets its **own budget**, so expensive generic terms can't eat the high-intent budget.

## Ad groups

- One **theme** per ad group (e.g. "mobile app development", "AI product development").
- Keywords in an ad group should all fit **the same ad and landing page**.
- If the ad would need to say something different, it's a different ad group.

## Keywords and match types

| Match type | Use |
|---|---|
| **Exact** | Proven, high-intent terms; most control |
| **Phrase** | Core themes with some reach |
| **Broad** | Only with Smart Bidding, solid conversion tracking and active negatives |

## Negative keywords

Start every account with a negative list, then add more weekly from the search terms report.

```
Starter negatives (adapt to the business):
jobs, careers, salary, internship, course, free, template, tutorial,
pdf, definition, what is, reddit, examples, DIY
```

- Keep **shared negative lists** at account level.
- Add **cross-negatives** so brand terms don't leak into generic campaigns.

## Responsive search ads

- Headlines: up to 30 characters each. Descriptions: up to 90.
- Include the keyword theme, the benefit, proof and a CTA across your headlines.
- Pin only when you must (e.g. a compliance line); pinning limits testing.
- Aim for a "Good" or "Excellent" ad strength without making ads generic.

## Weekly routine

1. Search terms report → add negatives, promote winners to exact
2. Check impression share lost to budget vs rank
3. Pause keywords spending with no conversions past your kill threshold
4. Review landing page conversion rate by ad group
